The Benefits of Professional HVAC Maintenance

Changing filters, clearing the outdoor unit, keeping registers open, and checking the drain are the homeowner's share of maintenance, and they matter. Everything beyond that needs a meter. Failures that leave you without heat or air rarely look like anything: an 80 percent capacitor resembles a healthy one, and a low charge is visible only in superheat and subcooling. A crack in the heat exchanger, a lazy flame signal, a pressure switch near its limit, or an amp draw drifting toward the breaker rating all stay hidden until someone measures them, and each is less expensive to handle a season early.

How to Prevent HVAC Maintenance Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Schedule maintenance in early spring and early fall, ahead of the busy season and the extreme weather
  • Jot down any unusual sounds or run times, since they help your technician diagnose early
  • Flush the condensate line every spring so algae cannot build up and clog it
  • If you have gas or other fuel-burning equipment, test your CO detectors every month
  • Trim plants back from the outdoor unit and give the coil a gentle rinse every spring
  • Change the filter every one to three months; it is the single highest-value habit there is
  • Leave every supply and return register open, including those in unused rooms
