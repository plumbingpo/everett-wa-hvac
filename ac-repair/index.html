When the air conditioning quits in Everett, WA, the house tells you fast, and so does everyone in it. The part that fails most often is the humble run capacitor: a few dollars of electronics that starts the compressor and fan, weakens quietly for years, and finally lets go on a brutal afternoon, leaving the outdoor unit humming but the fan still. Right behind it come pitted contactors that no longer pass power cleanly, condenser fan motors with dry bearings, refrigerant leaks that slowly bleed the system's capacity away, and evaporator coils that freeze into a block of ice whenever airflow collapses behind a clogged filter.

At Everett HVAC, every AC repair starts with measurement. Capacitors are metered against the rating on the label. Amp draws on the compressor and fan motor are compared to the data plate. Refrigerant charge is judged by superheat and subcooling, not by pressure readings alone, and any icing prompts a look at the evaporator coil, blower wheel, and filter. This matters because a single warm-air symptom can hide four different faults at four very different prices, and a refrigerant top-off that ignores the leak is just a short-term rental on cold air.

We service every common residential cooling system from all major brands, and our trucks stock the summer failure parts: capacitors, contactors, fan motors, hard-start kits, and condensate pumps, so most repairs are finished the same visit. When a compressor has truly failed on an aging system, we say so plainly and lay out the repair-versus-replace math honestly. The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

The Benefits of Professional AC Repair

An air conditioner packs several hazards into one cabinet. The capacitor can deliver a painful shock long after the breaker is off, the condensing unit is wired for 240 volts, and the refrigerant circuit is a sealed, pressurized loop that federal rules forbid venting. Servicing it properly takes gauges, a recovery machine, and a vacuum pump, not a DIY recharge can. Diagnosis has its own traps. A frozen coil is as likely to be an airflow issue as a charge issue, and recharging a system that still leaks wastes money while moisture and acid work on the compressor.

Professional service sees the full picture, not just the symptom. Our technicians meter electrical parts before replacing them, make charge decisions from measured superheat and subcooling, locate leaks with electronic detectors and dye, and check the filter, blower, and static pressure any time airflow could be the issue. The result is a repair that lasts the season instead of a guess that fails in three weeks. If your system is not worth saving, we will tell you directly, backed by numbers rather than a sales pitch.

AC Repair Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Keep supply and return vents open; closing rooms raises static pressure on the blower
  • Change the filter monthly during cooling season; starved airflow is how coils freeze
  • Shade is fine, but do not box in the outdoor unit with fences or covers
  • Keep shrubs two feet from the outdoor unit and gently rinse its coil each spring
  • Listen for new buzzing or clicking at startup, the classic sound of a dying capacitor
  • Flush the condensate drain line with the season's first use to head off clogs
  • Schedule a spring tune-up so weak parts are caught before summer arrives
