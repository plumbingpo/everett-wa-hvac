Think about the spaces your central system has always struggled with: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs too warm or too cold, or an older Everett home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the jobs a ductless mini split was made for. There is no ductwork to leak or lose energy, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a three-inch hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor varies its speed to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it runs as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Design is where mini split projects are won or lost, and Everett HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because an oversized head short-cycles, dehumidifies poorly, and hunts. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched sensibly across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and the choice between wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor units affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk every room with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, in finished line-hide outdoors.

Installation gets the same rigor as our largest systems. The line set is pressure-tested with nitrogen and evacuated to a micron-gauge-verified vacuum, and flare connections are torqued and leak-checked, since a weeping flare is the classic mini split failure. Condensate is drained or pumped reliably, and the charge is adjusted for line set length per the manufacturer's tables. We commission every zone in both heating and cooling, then hand you the remotes with the settings actually explained. Why Hire a Professional for Ductless Mini Split Installation?

Because a mini split looks like a simple box on a wall, a lot of them get installed like one, and they fail in predictable ways. The weakest link is the flare joint. Form one badly or leave it under-torqued and it seeps refrigerant slowly, so nobody notices until the head is blowing room-temperature air the next season. Skip the nitrogen test and the measured vacuum, and moisture stays in a circuit that inverter compressors do not forgive. Push the line set past the manufacturer's length or lift limits and you lose capacity without a single alarm. Forget the drain slope and the first muggy week leaves water marks on the drywall.

Sizing discipline matters even more here than with central systems, because the smallest available heads are already generous for many bedrooms; the wrong pick short-cycles, leaves humidity behind, and never settles. Our experienced technicians size each zone honestly, torque and leak-test every flare, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, set charge adjustments to the line length, and commission each head in both modes. Refrigerant work is federally regulated, and the electrical side needs a properly sized circuit and disconnect. The result is what a mini split should be: silent, efficient, and boring for fifteen years, instead of a mysterious underperformer from a rushed weekend install.

Maintenance Tips for Ductless Mini Split Installation

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Schedule professional cleaning of the indoor coil and blower wheel before buildup turns into growth
  • Clear snow, leaves, and clippings away from the outdoor unit year-round
  • Choose a setpoint and leave it alone, since inverters run best without constant on and off switching
  • Winter defrost pauses are expected; heavy ice buildup on the outdoor unit is your cue to call
  • Clean the washable filter screens in each head about once a month, since they clog faster than furnace filters
  • Check the condensate outlet during cooling season to make sure water is actually coming out
  • Book annual professional service to verify charge, flares, and drainage while fixes are cheap
